Log












    
    

        Builds an oxRNA2 structure of an RNA entirely in your browser (rigidRNA compiled to WebAssembly, in a background worker): rigid base-pair nodes per stem,
        de novo layout, Levenberg-Marquardt relaxation and export. The input is a sequence with its dot-bracket structure, a sequence alone (the engine predicts
        the structure), a PDB / mmCIF file or a design JSON. With Electrostatics the stems also feel oxRNA2's salt-dependent Debye-Hückel repulsion.
        The result opens as a normal system. The oxRNA2 molecular-dynamics relaxation of it is a separate step (oxDNA / oxpy), not part of the engine.
    





1. Structure





    
    
    

        
        
    

    

        

            
            
        

        

            
            
        

        

            
            
        

    

    

        
        
seqpred (WebAssembly, in your browser): nested minimum-free-energy structure with the Turner 2004 parameters and the salt correction of ViennaRNA 2.7, plus the kissing-loop / pseudoknot layer of the chosen single-chain model (auto router default; v2, v3, v4 = V3D, M2, h2d, ml2, ml3, NAT-1, ENERGY-1, ML-4, the multi-strand MS2 and MS-3 predictors and the domain-pairing tools where the loaded build carries them — the rest is disabled and says so; ml3 needs the pKiss program and runs natively only). Recommended: ML3 (native) or NAT-1 for natural RNA, v4 (V3D) or MS-3 for designed RNA; MS-3, domain pairing B and hybrid B are design decoders, not blind folding models; the auto router (recommended, the default) picks one frozen model per input and says which one answered and why; in the browser ML3 is not available, so natural RNA is answered by NAT-1 or the nested MFE and the result says so. A pasted reference structure (dot-bracket, pair list, bpseq or ct) is scored with the C++ metrics of the module. The result fills the dot-bracket field above. Free energies by the ViennaRNA 2.7.2 library (Lorenz et al., Institute for Theoretical Chemistry, University of Vienna), compiled to WebAssembly; seqpred is a C++ re-implementation of the rigidDNA2 Python predictors. Sequences longer than 600 nt get the nested MFE only (the long-range search takes minutes at 400 nt in one thread). A time budget bounds every stage: a stopped run says truncated and names the stage. After a prediction the summary names the model, the gate decision, the confidence status and the engine and loading times, and offers the result as dot-bracket, pairs and FASTA downloads.
